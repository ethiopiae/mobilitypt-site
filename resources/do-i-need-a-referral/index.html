The short answer

There are two separate questions. First, does the law let you see a physical therapist without a referral? Second, will your insurance pay for it without one? The answers can be different.

What California law allows

California allows "direct access" to physical therapy. That means a licensed physical therapist can evaluate and treat you without a doctor's referral or diagnosis first, within limits.

Under California Business and Professions Code section 2620.1, a physical therapist can treat you without a diagnosis for up to 45 calendar days or 12 visits, whichever comes first. After that, a physician and surgeon (or a podiatrist, for foot and ankle care) must sign your plan of care to keep going. {% if site.draft %}Placeholder Clinic to confirm current rules.{% endif %}

Your therapist will also refer you to a doctor if your symptoms suggest something that needs a doctor's care first.

What your insurance may require

Insurance rules are separate from the law, and they decide what your plan will pay for.

  • HMO and medical group plans usually need a referral and an authorization from your doctor or medical group before physical therapy is covered.
  • PPO plans and Medicare often don't need a referral to start, but coverage varies from plan to plan. Medicare also needs a doctor or other approved provider to sign off on your plan of care.
  • Auto MedPay and workers' compensation each have their own steps. Bring your claim information when you call.

You don't have to figure this out alone. Call us with your insurance card in hand and we'll check what your plan needs before your first visit.
