In an emergency, call 911. Go to the emergency room or call 911 for severe pain, numbness or weakness, trouble breathing, confusion, a bad headache, or loss of bladder or bowel control.

See a doctor first

After any accident, get checked by a doctor, even if you feel fine. A doctor can look for injuries that need medical care first and tell you when it's safe to start physical therapy.

Soreness can show up days later

It's common to feel okay at the scene and then notice neck pain, back pain, stiffness or headaches in the hours or days after. If new symptoms appear or get worse, let your doctor know.

How physical therapy may help

Physical therapy is often part of recovery from neck and back injuries such as whiplash and muscle strains. Depending on your injury, your therapist may use:

  • Gentle exercises to help you move more easily
  • Hands-on treatment
  • Strengthening and posture work to support your neck and back
  • A home program so you can keep improving between visits

Everyone heals differently. Your therapist will build a plan around your injury and your doctor's guidance, and adjust it as you go. Follow your therapist's and doctor's advice about what's safe for you.

Paying for care: auto MedPay

Many auto insurance policies include medical payments coverage, often called MedPay. It may cover physical therapy after a crash, no matter who was at fault. We accept auto MedPay from all carriers.

When you call to book, have your claim information ready:

  • Auto insurance company and claim number
  • Claims adjuster's name and phone number
  • Date of the accident
  • Your health insurance card, too

We can't give legal advice. For questions about your claim or settlement, talk to your insurer or attorney.
