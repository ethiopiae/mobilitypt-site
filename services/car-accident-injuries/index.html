Common injuries after a crash

Even a low-speed crash can strain the neck and back. Problems physical therapists commonly treat after car accidents include:

  • Whiplash and neck pain or stiffness
  • Low back and mid-back pain
  • Shoulder pain, including from the seat belt
  • Headaches that start in the neck, once your doctor has checked you
  • Knee, hip or wrist pain from bracing for the impact

What your visits look like

Your first visit is an evaluation. Your therapist asks about the accident and your symptoms, checks how you move, and explains what they find in plain words. Together you set goals for getting back to work, driving and daily life.

Visits then mix hands-on care, gentle exercises to restore motion, and strength work as you're ready. You also get a home program to keep progress going between visits. Read our guide on what to do after a car accident.

How long recovery takes

Recovery after a crash varies a lot from person to person. Your therapist will give you a plan after your evaluation and update it as you improve.

When to call a doctor or 911 instead

Call 911 or go to the emergency room after a crash if you have a severe headache, confusion, fainting, repeated vomiting, trouble speaking, chest pain, trouble breathing, or numbness or weakness in your arms or legs.

See a doctor before starting physical therapy, even if you feel fine. Call your doctor if your pain, dizziness or headaches get worse. This page is general information, not medical advice. Always follow your therapist or doctor's advice.

Insurance notes

We work with auto MedPay coverage from all carriers. When you call, have your auto insurance company name and claim number ready. We'll check what your policy covers before your first visit. Your health insurance may also cover physical therapy. See the plans we work with.
