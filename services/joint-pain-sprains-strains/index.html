Common joint and soft-tissue problems

A sore knee, a rolled ankle or a stiff shoulder can get in the way of work, sleep and the things you enjoy. Problems physical therapists commonly treat include:

  • Ankle sprains and other ligament sprains
  • Muscle strains, such as a pulled hamstring, calf or back muscle
  • Knee pain, including pain around the kneecap
  • Shoulder pain and stiffness, including rotator cuff problems
  • Hip pain
  • Elbow, wrist and hand pain from overuse, such as tennis elbow
  • Joint stiffness and pain from arthritis
  • Sports injuries

What your visits look like

Your first visit is an evaluation. Your therapist asks how the problem started, checks your motion, strength and balance, and explains what they find in plain words. Together you set goals that matter to you.

Visits then mix hands-on care with exercises to restore motion and build strength. As you improve, your program moves toward the activities you want to get back to, and you get a home program to keep progress going between visits.

How long recovery takes

Every injury and every person is different. Your therapist will give you a plan after your evaluation and update it as you improve.

When to call a doctor or 911 instead

Get emergency care or call 911 if a joint looks out of place or bent, a bone may be broken, or the limb is cold, pale, or numb.

See your doctor soon if you can't put weight on the leg or use the arm, swelling is severe or keeps getting worse, or a joint is red, hot and swollen, especially with a fever. This page is general information, not medical advice. Always follow your therapist or doctor's advice.

Insurance notes

We work with Medicare Part B, many PPO plans and several local medical groups. If your plan is an HMO or medical group, you'll likely need a referral from your doctor first. We'll check your coverage before your first visit. See the plans we work with or read whether you need a referral.
