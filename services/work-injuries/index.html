Common work injuries

Injuries on the job happen in offices, warehouses, job sites and everywhere in between. Problems physical therapists commonly treat include:

  • Back strains from lifting, bending or twisting
  • Shoulder injuries from reaching overhead or carrying loads
  • Knee and ankle injuries from slips, trips and falls
  • Neck and upper-back pain from long hours at a desk or computer
  • Repetitive strain problems in the wrist, hand and elbow
  • Recovery after surgery for a work injury

What your visits look like

Your first visit is an evaluation. Your therapist asks how you got hurt and what your job asks of your body, checks how you move, and explains what they find in plain words. Together you set goals around your daily life and your work tasks.

Visits then mix hands-on care, exercises to rebuild strength and motion, and practice with the movements your job needs, like lifting safely. You also get a home program to keep progress going between visits.

How long recovery takes

Every injury and every job is different. Your therapist will give you a plan after your evaluation and update it as you improve. Your doctor decides when you can return to work.

When to call a doctor or 911 instead

Call 911 for a serious injury at work, such as a fall from height, a head injury, heavy bleeding, a possible broken bone, chest pain, trouble breathing, or new loss of bladder or bowel control.

See a doctor soon if you have numbness, tingling or weakness, or pain that keeps getting worse. This page is general information, not medical advice. Always follow your therapist or doctor's advice.

Insurance notes

We accept workers' compensation. When you call, please have ready:

  • Your claim number
  • Your claims adjuster's name and phone number
  • Your employer's name and contact information
  • Your doctor's referral or prescription for physical therapy

Treatment may need authorization from the claims adjuster before your visits start. We'll help check what your claim needs. See all the plans we work with.
