Rehab after orthopedic surgery

After surgery, physical therapy helps you get back motion, strength and confidence step by step, within the limits your surgeon sets. Surgeries that physical therapists commonly help people recover from include:

  • Knee and hip replacement
  • ACL and other knee ligament or meniscus repair
  • Shoulder surgery, such as rotator cuff repair
  • Spine surgery, when your surgeon clears you for therapy
  • Foot, ankle, hand and wrist surgery, including after a fracture

What your visits look like

At your first visit, your therapist reviews your surgeon's instructions, checks your swelling, motion and strength, and asks about your daily life and goals. Together you build a plan that follows your surgeon's protocol.

Visits then move forward in stages: gentle motion first, then strength, balance and the everyday tasks that matter to you, like stairs, driving or getting back to work. You also get a home program for the days between visits.

How long recovery takes

It depends on your surgery, your health and your surgeon's protocol. Your therapist will give you a plan after your evaluation and update it as you improve.

When to call a doctor or 911 instead

Call 911 if you have sudden shortness of breath, chest pain, or you cough up blood. These can be signs of a blood clot in the lungs.

Call your surgeon right away if you notice pain, swelling, warmth or redness in your calf, a fever, or redness, warmth, drainage or a bad smell at your incision. Also call if your pain suddenly gets much worse. This page is general information, not medical advice. Always follow your surgeon, doctor or therapist's advice.
